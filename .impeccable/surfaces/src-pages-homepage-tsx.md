Scope: the public site's home page and services page (Spanish at `/` and `/servicios/`, English at `/en/` and `/en/services/`). Visitor mode: Persuade.

Audience: a driver in Hamilton Township or the next town over, on a phone, car already misbehaving, often reading Spanish first. Job: find this shop and walk in; call when they'd rather ask first. Proof on hand: 20+ years, the owner works the cars himself, Spanish spoken, walk-ins, same-day on common jobs, free estimates, a Google profile with 6 reviews. No photos, no logo, no testimonials, and no transmission work.

Constraints: name, address and phone must match the Business Profile exactly, "Suite C" included. Content ships pre-rendered in both languages. No invented reviews, ratings, or numbers.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The site is the shop's service record: the work order a mechanic fills in, printed on the paper it lives on. It keeps the arrangement a customer expects from an auto shop site, because the user chose the category standard, and earns its distinction from material rather than from structure. It refuses the category's palette and volume: no corporate blue, no red-on-white urgency, no stock photo of a smiling man with a clipboard, no shouting.

OWN-WORLD: Oat paper ground with graphite ink. Hairline ruled rows and ruled blocks, the way a form is ruled. One oxblood accent, spent only on the phone and the primary action. Headings in a grotesque with a point of view, body in a plain workhorse, and form labels and every figure in a typewriter face with tabular numerals, because hours, part codes and phone numbers are measurements. Panels are off-white leaves laid on the paper with a single hairline, no heavy borders and no offset shadows. Carbon-copy tint alternates on ruled rows.

STORY: The visitor understands in one screen that a mechanic with twenty years on the job works here, that he speaks their language, where Suite C is, and that he is open right now. They believe it because the page reads like the shop's own paperwork: specific, filled in, unexaggerated. Then they tap the number or tap directions.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Oat paper, ruled hairline under a quiet header carrying the wordmark, two nav items, the language toggle and a small phone button. Left two thirds: the shop name set large in graphite, "Taller mecánico en Hamilton Township, NJ" beneath it, a two-line lede, and a ruled trust row reading 20+ años · sin cita · mismo día · presupuestos gratis in the typewriter face. The oxblood call button sits under it at the largest weight on the page, with the open-now state as a small ruled stamp beside today's hours. Right third: a ruled record block, the address with "Suite C" on its own ruled line, hours as a ruled table with tabular figures, and beneath it the photo slot, drawn as a labelled ruled frame until the client sends images. No photo, no texture, no ornament stands in for the missing picture.

FORM: The Service Record, my remaining conventional grounded candidate, fused with the canon by the user's explicit choice in the safer register (re-roll round 2, assignment suspended). Seed key 5e00df4a. The canon supplies the arrangement: photo hero slot, service cards in a grid, reviews, makes, areas served, hours and map, closing call. The Service Record supplies every material decision. Craft bar: the local Mercer County shops this competes with, beaten on clarity, legibility and trust rather than on polish, since there are no photographs to out-polish anyone with.

Signature interaction: the open-now stamp prints itself beside today's hours after load, read from the shop's clock in America/New_York. Motion grammar: one authored moment per page, exponential ease-out from an already-visible default, honored against reduced-motion.

Unresolved: real shop photos and a logo (the photo slot is coded and labelled until files arrive), and the final About and Mission wording, which stays placeholder copy to confirm with the client.
