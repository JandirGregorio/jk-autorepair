Scope: the public site's home page and services page (Spanish at `/` and `/servicios/`, English at `/en/` and `/en/services/`). Visitor mode: Persuade.

Audience: a driver in Hamilton Township or the next town over, on a phone, car already misbehaving, often reading Spanish first. Job: find this shop and walk in; call when they'd rather ask first. Proof on hand: 20+ years, the owner works the cars himself, Spanish spoken, walk-ins, same-day on common jobs, free estimates, a Google profile with 6 reviews. No photos, no logo, no testimonials, and no transmission work.

Constraints: name, address and phone must match the Business Profile exactly, "Suite C" included. Content ships pre-rendered in both languages. No invented reviews, ratings, or numbers.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The site is the shop's hand-painted sign, scaled to a screen. A painted sign exists to be read from across a street by someone already moving, so it says who, what, where, and the number, in that order, at full voice. It refuses the category arrangement: photo hero of a clean bay, a row of identical service cards, a review strip, a map dropped at the bottom.

OWN-WORLD: Enamel on metal. Deep sign blue ground, sign yellow and a hot red as the two painted accents, off-white for painted fields. Display lettering in a signage face with a hard offset drop shadow, a brush script reserved for one painted line ("Se habla español"), body text in a plain workhorse grotesk that holds Spanish diacritics. Painted arrows point at real destinations. Edges are hand-cut, never soft shadows; panels butt against each other like painted boards. Every rule is a brushstroke width, not a hairline.

STORY: The visitor understands in one screen that this is a neighborhood mechanic with twenty years on the job who speaks their language, that he is on East State Street in Suite C, and that he is open right now. They believe it because the sign is specific, not because it says "trusted". Then they either tap the number or tap directions and drive over.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Full-bleed sign blue. The shop name painted large across the top with its offset shadow, "TALLER MECÁNICO" set beneath it in the same painted voice. A brush-script "Se habla español" runs at an angle across the lower left of the name block. The right or lower half is one painted address plate: "1865 E State St" with "Suite C" on its own line at equal weight, Hamilton Township below it, and a painted arrow aimed at a directions link. The phone number sits as the largest single element on the page, lettered inside a hot red painted button that says "Llamar" beside it, with the open-now state painted as a small enamel badge next to today's hours. Nothing is decorative: the four things a stranger needs are the four largest things on the sign.

FORM: El Rótulo, candidate 1 of my ordered grounded list, chosen by the user over the roll's assigned candidate 3 (Shop Manual Plate) from the IMPECCABLE'S PICK card. Seed key 5e00df4a. Raises carried in from the hand it beat: callouts and labels speak the driver's problem in plain Spanish rather than naming parts; one painted object owns the first viewport instead of a hero band stacked over sections; the sign's painted geometry rules the layout rather than a grid of equal boxes; every figure is held by one committed ink outline and the page closes on a hand-ruled title panel.

Signature interaction: the open-now enamel badge lights after hydration from the shop's own clock, and the painted arrow travels a few pixels toward its destination on hover and focus. Motion grammar: one authored moment per page, exponential ease-out from an already-visible default, honored against reduced-motion.
